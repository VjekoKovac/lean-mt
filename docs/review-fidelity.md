## Independent review of the Section 5 proof

I compared the actual theorem body in `Solution.lean` and the interpolation lemma in `MT/Transfer.lean` with Section 5. The choice of $C$ precedes the choice of surface. Writing $a=(1+2/\beta)\log C>0$, the proof sets $\alpha=\log 2/(2a)>0$. For fixed $S$, the analytic assumption supplies $\kappa,b,H_0$. There is an integer $N$ such that $C^N>H_0$. For $n\ge N$, the degree $k=2^n$ satisfies $k\ge n$, so amplification at $k$ can be used in transfer at $H=C^k$. This gives $b2^k$ at radius $\kappa\exp(a k)$.

For $R\ge\kappa\exp(a2^N)$, the proof brackets $\log(R/\kappa)/a$ between $2^n$ and $2^{n+1}$. The upper bracket implies $(R/\kappa)^\alpha\le2^{2^n}$; the lower bracket and radius monotonicity give the desired estimate at $R$. The prefactor is $b/\kappa^\alpha$, and enlarging the threshold to at least one preserves the claim. This resolves the endpoint at the initial sampled radius and gives every large real radius. The final core-to-full inclusion keeps the same prefactor and radius threshold for all admissible extension realizations. I found no appeal in this reduction to either unnamed assumptions or a theorem equivalent to the conclusion. Both premises appear in its type. The final solution discharges them with the two proposition proofs.
