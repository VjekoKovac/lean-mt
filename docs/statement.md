# Reading the theorem statement

Open `Challenge.lean` at `MT.PowerLoss`. Each `∀` reads “for every”, each `∃` reads “there exists”, and `→` introduces a hypothesis. Trace the quantifiers from left to right. First fix a real $\beta$ with $0<\beta\le1$. At this point one must choose a single $\alpha>0$. Only then is the dimension $d$ introduced, followed by the surface $\Sigma$. The multiplier $c>0$ and threshold $R_0\ge1$ can depend on that surface. The inequality must hold at every real $R\ge R_0$.

The source is Theorem 1.3 in the six authors' [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22465v1). The table uses names from the `MT` namespace.

## Why two constants appear

The paper allows noncompact surfaces. An $L^2$ function on such a surface need not admit an absolutely convergent Fourier integral. To avoid assigning it a value by the convention for a divergent Bochner integral, the proof uses the paper's integrable test functions. The theorem is stated for every realization of the full operator agreeing almost everywhere with that integral on $L^1\cap L^2$. Multiplication by $w(x)\,dx$ preserves almost-everywhere equality.

The checked lemma `localConstant_le_full` places each core ratio in the full supremum. The lemma `extension_agreesOnCore` shows this class of realizations is inhabited. These facts let a proof use the chart-supported inputs without a global regularity, extension, or compactness hypothesis. For arbitrary locally integrable weight representatives, mathlib's nonnegative integral is total even on a nonmeasurable line trace. The lower-bound proof uses only Borel weights, where this convention is immaterial.

The source says $0<\alpha_\beta\ll 1$. Its asserted quantitative content is the existence of a positive exponent uniform over dimensions and surfaces. With $R\ge1$, a proved exponent can always be replaced by a smaller positive one. Section 5 provides the specific $\log 2/[2(1+2/\beta)\log C]$. The numerical $10^{-5}$ assertion for $C^3$ surfaces after the theorem is a distinct remark.

## A first proof to follow

Open `MT/Constant.lean` at `localConstant_mono`. If $R\le R'$, a weight whose support lies in $B_R$ also has support in $B_{R'}$. The proof keeps the same input, weight, and numerical ratio. The line `apply sSup_le_sSup` reduces monotonicity of the two suprema to inclusion of their sets of ratios. This is the first inequality in the proof of Section 5.

The source-to-coordinate interpretation in this guide is an AI statement review. The Lean kernel checks the declarations and supplied proofs. It cannot certify that the definitions express the intended theorem. A human author reviewing the challenge should in particular confirm the surface area convention, the interpretation of the two X-ray suprema, and the local graph condition.

## From the chart to the weighted integral

The paper's Lemma 3.3 uses a parabolic slice at a curved point. In `MT/Taylor.lean`, `HasCurvature.exists_direction` first supplies a direction with a nonzero quadratic coefficient. The proof uses Schwarz symmetry of the Hessian and polarization. The lemma `GraphChart.exists_taylor_bound` estimates the error after subtracting the value, first derivative, and quadratic form at an arbitrary point in the chart. It applies mathlib's mean-value theorem twice to the restriction of the height along a line. The corollary `HasCurvature.exists_parabolic_slice` finds an interval of that line whose graph lies in the surface. Subtracting its tangent and dividing by its quadratic coefficient gives the standard parabola with an error bounded by a constant times the stated Hölder power.

A useful choice when constructing the actual input is to take it constant on each small surface patch. The kernel in Lemma 3.4 is positive on resonant patch pairs and nonnegative everywhere. Consequently the numerator only needs a lower bound on each patch's surface area, while the input norm needs an upper bound. This gives the same power estimate as the paper's division of the input by the graph Jacobian. The checked statement `resonant_patch_mass` allows an arbitrary surface measure satisfying the explicit patch mass and phase bounds. Its cube specialization is `resonant_box_mass`. The concrete patches and their input norms are handled by `GraphChart.cap_test_data` in `MT/Caps.lean`. In `MT/ChartPhase.lean`, `GraphChart.resonant_phases` bounds the phases on all pairs of caps whose two arithmetic sums agree. Finally `cap_weightedMass_lower_linear` in `MT/SurfacePackets.lean` identifies the packet sum with the cap extension evaluated at the adjoint of the frequency map. Its proof integrates the exact product Fourier transform of the triangular cutoff and retains every resonant pair.
