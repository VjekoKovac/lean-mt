# From an energy configuration to a power loss

The original source is Cairo, Gottlieb Fenves, Kovač, Oliveira, Zakharov and Zhang, [arXiv:2611.22465v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22465v1). This guide covers its Theorem 1.3, Proposition 3.1, Remark 3.2, Lemmas 3.3–3.5, and §5. The complete analytic transfer is the unconditional Lean theorem `MT.analyticTransfer : MT.AnalyticTransfer`. The source’s individual proof steps and the implementation choices are described below. [Build and axiom report](../checks/analytic-axioms.txt)

## What the theorem quantifies

1. Fix a real Hölder exponent $0<β≤1$. One positive exponent $α$ must work simultaneously for every positive intrinsic dimension $d$ and every eligible surface. The harmless notation $α\ll1$ can always be arranged by decreasing a proved exponent when $R≥1$.
2. The surface $Σ\subseteq\mathbb R^{d+1}$ is locally a hypersurface of class $C^{2,β}$. At each point a rigid coordinate change exhibits a $C^2$ graph. For a chart-dependent constant $K$, its Hessian satisfies $\|D^2φ(u)-D^2φ(v)\|≤K|u-v|^β$ locally. At least one point has nonzero second fundamental form. A symmetric form can be nonzero even if its determinant vanishes. Connectedness only enters the motivating discussion.
3. Surface measure is Euclidean area $dσ$. For frequency point $ξ$ and physical point $y$, the kernel is $e(ξ\cdot y)=\exp(2πi\,ξ\cdot y)$. For a unit $ν$, $Xw(ν)$ is the supremum, over $v\perpν$, of $\int_{\mathbb R}w(v+tν)\,dt$. Offsets here are pointwise. The $L^\infty$ norm in direction is taken on the unit sphere. A bound on all individual lines implies the required essential bound.
4. The quotient has numerator $\int|\mathcal E_Σ f|^2w$ and denominator $\|f\|_{L^2(dσ)}^2\|Xw\|_\infty$. Take the supremum over $L^2(dσ)$ data and nonnegative locally integrable weights supported in the origin-centred radius-$R$ ball. The constructed weights have continuous representatives. Excluding zero denominators removes undefined ratios. Each witness below has finite positive denominator.
5. For each such surface there are $c_Σ>0$, $R_Σ≥1$ such that $c_ΣR^α≤\mathcal C_Σ(R)$ for **every real** $R≥R_Σ$. On noncompact surfaces, $L^2$ alone does not guarantee absolute convergence of the extension integral. The construction uses bounded compactly supported chart data in $L^1\cap L^2$. The Lean comparison in `MT/Constant.lean` allows every full $L^2$ realization agreeing on these integrable tests. No extension theorem for general $L^2$ data is implied.

## Proposition 3.1 and its constants

Let $H$ be sufficiently large, $L≥1$, and let finite nonempty sets $A\subset[-1,1]$, $B\subset[-1,1]^2$ satisfy

- $|a-a'|≥1/H$ for distinct $a,a'\in A$.
- For every $s\in\mathbb R$ and every interval $I$ of length $1/H$, at most $L$ **points** $(b,τ)\in B$ satisfy $τ-2sb\in I$. Coincident projected values retain their multiplicities. This is a bound on *vertical* width, including arbitrarily large finite slopes.
- $E(A,B)$ counts ordered $(a,a',ζ,ζ')\in A^2\times B^2$ for which $(a,a^2)+ζ=(a',a'^2)+ζ'$. Diagonal solutions contribute.

For fixed $Σ,β$, choose positive constants $H_0,κ,c_T$ independently of $H,L,A,B$. For all the above inputs with $H≥H_0$, Proposition 3.1 gives
$$c_T E(A,B)/(L\,|A|\,|B|)≤\mathcal C_Σ(κH^{1+2/β}).$$
The same $κ$ and $c_T$ are used at every scale. This uniformity is needed in §5.

### Lemma 3.3 and the chart measure

Choose a point where the second fundamental form is nonzero. Move it to the origin, put its tangent plane horizontally, and diagonalize its real symmetric Hessian. At least one eigenvalue is nonzero. Reflect the final coordinate if necessary to make the first quadratic coefficient positive. A fixed invertible linear scaling and restriction of the chart give, on $[-1,1]^d$,
$$φ(ξ)=\sum_{j=1}^n λ_jξ_j^2+r(ξ),\qquad 1≤n≤d,\quad λ_1=1,\quad |λ_j|=1,\quad |r(ξ)|≤K|ξ|^{2+β}.$$
The Taylor remainder follows by integrating the difference $D^2φ(tξ)-D^2φ(0)$ along the segment. The Lean lemmas `MT.graph_taylor_remainder` and `MT.GraphChart.exists_taylor_bound` prove this estimate before diagonalizing the quadratic form. `MT.HasCurvature.exists_direction` uses symmetry and polarization to obtain a nonisotropic vector. This argument uses Hölder continuity of the second derivative. For a graph, $J(ξ)=\sqrt{1+|\nabla φ(ξ)|^2}$ lies between positive chart constants on a smaller closed cube. A parameter function $f_0$ corresponds to $f_0/J$ on the graph, extended by zero on the rest of $Σ$. Its extension is the integral against $dξ$, and its squared $L^2(dσ)$ norm is $\int|f_0|^2/J$. An equivalent choice for this lower bound is to take the constant value of $f_0$ on each surface patch and integrate with its positive area density. This only needs upper and lower bounds for patch areas. The kernel retains the same sign with positive surface measure. `MT.GraphChart.local_area_bounds` supplies uniform area comparisons using Lipschitz estimates for the graph map and its inverse on the graph.

The checked proof only needs one curved direction. In `MT.HasCurvature.exists_oriented_chart` a reflection sends the chosen coordinate vector to a nonisotropic unit vector. Write $E$ for this orthogonal map, $u$ for the base point, and $q=D^2φ(u)[E e_1,E e_1]/2\ne0$. In `MT.normalizedHeight` the new height is $[φ(u+Ex)-φ(u)-Dφ(u)[Ex]]/q$. It has first-axis coefficient one for either sign of $q$. For $z=ha e_1$ and $x$ in the cap, the difference of the two Hessian quadratic values costs at most $\lVert D^2φ(u)\rVert(\lVert x\rVert+\lVert z\rVert)\lVert x-z\rVert$. This bounds all mixed and transverse terms at once. The checked `MT.GraphChart.cube_height_bound` gives $|φ_{\mathrm{norm}}(x)-(ha)^2|≤P h^{2+β}+Q hε$ on every small cap, with fixed nonnegative $P,Q$.

Changing frequency coordinates by $ξ\mapsto Mξ+v$ sends physical coordinates by $M^{\mathsf T}$ and introduces a unit-modulus phase. Jacobians for surface integrals, ambient volumes, line arclengths, and the enclosing ball must all be accounted for. Their costs are bounded by constants depending on the fixed affine chart. `MT.GraphChart.frequencyCoordinates_point` constructs the invertible ambient linear part $M$ and identifies the graph point after subtracting its base point. Translation cancels in the pairwise frequency differences inside the expanded squared modulus. The argument of the original extension becomes $M^{\mathsf T}y$, as proved in `MT/SurfacePackets.lean`.

### Parameters and witnesses

One concrete choice realizing the comparisons in equation (3.1) is
$$h=c_h H^{-1/β},\quad Y=H/h,\quad T=H/h^2,\quad ε=c_ε h/H.$$
Choose $c_h,c_ε\in(0,1)$ small depending on the chart, $β$, and $d$, then $H$ sufficiently large. In particular $Yε=c_ε$, $Thε=c_ε$, $Tε^2=c_ε^2/H$, and $Th^{2+β}=c_h^β$. Since $β≤1$ and $H≥1$, $hH≤1$. The latter inequality is needed for nearly horizontal lines. The thirteen scale bounds and identities are `MT.transferScales_bounds`. The actual weight, open ball radius, strict positivity, and norm bound at these choices are combined by `MT.counterWeight_at_transfer_scale`.

Set $Q_a=ha e_1+[-ε,ε]^d$, $f_0=ε^{-d/2}\sum_{a\in A}1_{Q_a}$. Choose $2c_ε<1$ so the boxes are disjoint and contained in the chart for large $H$. Then $\int|f_0|^2\,dξ=2^d|A|$, giving $\|f\|_2^2\asymp_Σ |A|$. `MT.capTest_input` and `MT.GraphChart.cap_test_data` prove the analogous estimate for the actual surface-area test. It gives a chart radius and a uniform $K$, exact cube volume, measurable disjoint caps, cap areas between $(2ε)^d$ and $K^d(2ε)^d$, membership in $L^1\cap L^2$, and $0<2^d|A|≤\|f\|_2^2≤K^d2^d|A|$. The conclusion applies after any specified orthogonal parameter rotation and translation of the origin to the selected chart point. With $χ(u)=\max(1-|u|,0)$ use the continuous nonnegative weight
$$w(x,t)=χ(t/T)\prod_{j=1}^d χ(x_j/Y)
\left|\sum_{(b,τ)\in B}e(hbx_1+h^2τt)\right|^2.$$
Its support lies in the box $[-Y,Y]^d\times[-T,T]$. Enlarge the open ball by a fixed factor to include the closed support, also after changing coordinates. Its radius is at most $κH^{1+2/β}$. The concrete witness is `MT.counterWeight`. Its continuity, nonnegativity, exact value $w(0)=|B|^2$, and admissibility in the open ball of radius $(\sqrt{d+1}+1)T$ for $0<Y≤T$ are proved in `MT/LineEstimates.lean`. The X-ray norm is strictly positive by continuity at zero; the computed direction-space essential supremum uses `MT.sphereArea_pos`. That lemma proves positive $d$-dimensional area of the sphere by projecting it onto a set containing the open unit $d$-ball.

### Lemma 3.4 and the resonant phase

The Fourier transform of $χ$ is $η(s)=(\sin(πs)/(πs))^2$, continuously extended by $η(0)=1$. The exact complex Fourier transform is `MT.fourier_tent`. The Lean proof identifies the tent with the convolution of the indicator of a centered unit interval with itself, via the length of the overlap of the two intervals. It reuses Mathlib's `Real.fourier_mul_convolution_eq`, `integral_exp_mul_I_eq_sinc`, and `intervalIntegral.integral_comp_mul_right`. The transform is everywhere nonnegative and bounded below on a fixed interval about zero. Expanding both squared moduli and using Fubini gives a sum over $A^2\times B^2$ with prefactor $ε^{-d}$. Bounded compact support and finite sums give absolute integrability here. On $Q_a\times Q_{a'}$ the kernel is
$$Y^dT\,η(Y(ξ_1-ξ'_1+h(b-b')))\,η(T(φ(ξ)-φ(ξ')+h^2(τ-τ')))\prod_{j=2}^dη(Y(ξ_j-ξ'_j)).$$
The checked product Fourier identity and the integrated packet inequality are in `MT/PacketKernel.lean`. In particular, `MT.packet_mass_ge_energy` proves the lower bound for a sum of complex characters integrated against finite patch measures. `MT.tentFactor_lower` proves the explicit kernel bound $η(z)≥1/4$ on $|z|≤1/3$. Discard nonresonant ordered quadruples by nonnegativity. Write $ξ=ha e_1+u$ and $ξ'=ha'e_1+u'$. The first argument cancels to $Y(u_1-u'_1)$, bounded by $2Yε$. In the quadratic difference, energy cancels the $h^2$ term. The two remaining first-coordinate terms are $2h(au_1-a'u'_1)+u_1^2-(u'_1)^2$. This is `MT.resonant_phase`. All other quadratic terms contribute $O_d(ε^2)$. The two Taylor remainders cost $O_Σ(h^{2+β})$ because $|ξ|,|ξ'|=O_d(h)$. In the Lean version `MT.GraphChart.resonant_phases` chooses $c_h=c_ε=c$ using continuity of $Pc^β+Qc$ at zero and the available chart radius. Simultaneously $c≤1/12$, all caps fit in the chart, and every kernel argument has absolute value at most $1/3$ for every $H≥1$. This is proved for all configurations of the original theorem. Thus the time argument is bounded by $O_Σ(T(hε+ε^2+h^{2+β}))$. Choose the two small constants so every argument stays inside the positivity interval. Each resonant kernel is then at least $c_ΣY^dT$. Integrating over the two cubes and multiplying by $ε^{-d}$ gives
$$\|\mathcal E_Σ f\|_{L^2(w)}^2≥c_Σ(Yε)^d T E(A,B)\gtrsim_Σ T E(A,B).$$

### Lemma 3.5 and the supremum over lines

For positive $Z$ take a finite indexed family of real frequencies $ω_i$ with at most $L$ indices in every interval of length $Z^{-1}$, and coefficients $|c_i|=1$. Expanding the square integrated against $χ(t/Z)$ gives a matrix with entries $Zη(Z(ω_i-ω_j))$. For each fixed $i$, group the $j$ by the integer $n=\lfloor |Z(ω_i-ω_j)|\rfloor$. The two signed pieces of each shell contain at most $2L$ indices in total. In shell zero use $η≤1$, and in later shells use $η≤1/(πn)^2$. A uniform majorant is $6(1/(n+1)-1/(n+2))$ for all shells. Summing the telescoping differences gives at most 1. Applying the triangle inequality, the integral is at most $12ZL|B|$. The finite regrouping and telescoping identity are `MT.kernel_shell_sum` and `MT.shell_tail_sum`. The estimate for the actual sinc-squared kernel is `MT.sinc_row_le`. It uses Mathlib's kernel-proved inequality `Real.pi_gt_three` to bound the decay. `MT.tent_large_sieve` proves the one-dimensional integral inequality with every parameter above, via the exact Fourier transform, the finite matrix expansion, and a change of variables. The scaled inequality is exactly equation (3.4). The proof splits into the following two line directions.

Write a unit direction as $(v,q)\in\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R$ and parametrize the whole line by arclength from $y$.

- If $|q|≥1/\sqrt2$, substitute $t=y_{d+1}+s q$, discard spatial cutoffs bounded by one, and take $Z=T$. The frequencies become $h^2(τ+(v_1/(qh))b)$, with coefficients of modulus one. Occupancy applies with slope $-v_1/(2qh)$ and width $(Th^2)^{-1}=H^{-1}$. The line mass is $O(TL|B|)$.
- If $|q|≤1/\sqrt2$, some $|v_j|≥1/\sqrt{2d}$. Retain only $χ(x_j/Y)$ and bound the square of the sum by $|B|^2$. Integrating gives $O_d(Y|B|^2)$. Use slope zero and cover $[-1,1]$ by $O(H)$ intervals of length $1/H$ to obtain $|B|\lesssim LH$. Since $YH/T=hH≤1$, this line also has mass $O_Σ(TL|B|)$.

The complete line and sphere-norm estimates are `MT.counterWeight_line_bound` and `MT.counterWeight_xrayNorm`. The Lean proof chooses a coordinate $i$ with $|v_i|≥1/(d+1)$ for the unit line direction, then distinguishes whether that coordinate is temporal or spatial. This proves the source estimate with explicit constant $12(d+1)$. The covering bound used on spatial lines is `MT.Configuration.card_le_three_mul`, namely $|B|≤3HL$ when $H≥1$. These bounds hold in the normalized coordinates. In the original physical variables the test weight is $w'(x)=w(D^{-1}x)$, with $D=M^{\mathsf T}$. Both support radius and line bound acquire at most the factor $\lVert D\rVert$. The weighted mass acquires the factor $|\det D|$. `MT.inverse_counterWeight_estimates` and `MT.weightedMass_inverse_weight` prove these facts, including positive X-ray norm after the change. [Physical change of variables](physical-change.md)

Divide the weighted mass lower bound by the two norm upper bounds to obtain Proposition 3.1. The real scalar step, with constants $c_E,c_F,c_X>0$, is `MT.transfer_quotient`, whose prefactor is $c_E/(c_Fc_X)$. For the actual integral and configuration types, `MT.localConstant_of_transfer_estimates` turns the three bounds, weight admissibility and positive denominator into the claimed estimate for the supremum. The proof of `MT.analyticTransfer` supplies all the inputs to this step. In `MT.graph_cap_weightedMass_lower` the cap amplitude, two areas and kernel factor give exactly $(Yε)^dT/4$ times the energy. Put $N=\lVert D\rVert$, $J=|\det D|$, and let $K$ be the local graph Lipschitz constant in the two-sided area estimate. The implemented choices are
$$
H_0=1,\quad
κ=\frac{N(\sqrt{d+1}+1)}{c^2},\quad
c_E=\frac{Jc^d}{4},\quad c_F=2^dK^d+1,\quad c_X=12N(d+1),\quad
b=\frac{c_E}{c_F c_X}.
$$
The choices are fixed for the chart, intrinsic dimension and smoothness before any $H,L,A,B$. The squared input norm is bounded by $c_F|A|$ and the X-ray norm by $c_X LT|B|$. Both are positive and finite. The lower bound for the weighted mass is $c_E T E(A,B)$. If a larger fixed $κ$ is desired in the source’s $κ\gg_Σ1$ notation, `MT.localConstant_mono` gives the bound at every larger radius.

## How §5 fills all the radii

Proposition 4.1 supplies an absolute $C>1$ before any $β,d,Σ$ are chosen. For each $k=2^n$, $n\in\mathbb N$, use its $A_k,B_k,L_k$ at $H=C^k$. The resulting sample lower bound is $b_Σ2^k$ at radius $κC^{(1+2/β)k}$, for sufficiently large $n$. Here $b_Σ>0$ is the transfer constant. The factor $L_k$ has already canceled in the normalized energy. Put $a=(1+2/β)\log C>0$. For $R$ beyond the first valid sample, bracket $\log(R/κ)/a$ between $2^n$ and $2^{n+1}$. The lower bound on this logarithm gives a sample radius no greater than $R$ and an admissible index. The upper bound gives $(R/κ)^{\log 2/(2a)}≤2^{2^n}$. Monotonicity in the ball radius completes the argument with explicit lower-bound multiplier $b_Σ/κ^α$. The factor $2$ in the denominator of the exponent comes from consecutive **degrees** $k,2k$.

`MT.paperExponent_pos` and `MT.powerLoss_of_dyadic_samples` implement this passage, with the dyadic samples as hypotheses. The latter reuses Mathlib's Archimedean bracketing lemma `exists_nat_pow_near`, its real log and exp monotonicity, and real power identities. All real-power and logarithm estimates in this passage use exact real analysis. The subsequent manuscript remark about $10^{-5}$ for $C^3$ surfaces is outside Theorem 1.3.
