## Output, definitions and quantifiers

In Proposition “Arithmetic amplification” (prop:amplification), there is one absolute real C>1. For every dyadic integer k choose finite nonempty sets A⊂[−1,1], B⊂[−1,1]² and positive Lₖ. Distinct elements of A are at least C⁻ᵏ apart. For every s∈ℝ and every interval I of length C⁻ᵏ, at most Lₖ points (b,τ)∈B have τ−2sb∈I. Closed intervals suffice by inclusion. There is an absolute b>1 such that Lₖ≤bᵏ. The energy satisfies E(A,B)≥2ᵏLₖ|A||B|.

Here P(a)=(a,a²). Energy counts ordered (a,ζ,a′,ζ′)∈A×B×A×B satisfying P(a)+ζ=P(a′)+ζ′. The same count defines E_K over a number field or coordinatewise on its diagonal embedding. The width is vertical for every slope, including arbitrarily large slopes.

## Parameters, tower and lattice counts

Fix the discriminant bound D>1 first. The notation X≲≈Y of the arithmetic section allows X≤b(D)ᵏY. Each occurrence can have its own base b(D)>1, independent of m, ρ, S, C and k. Multiplying finitely many losses multiplies their bases. Choose m only after finding that the full product of these bases is independent of m.

* **Field tower (lemma:towerdata).** The manuscript states the existence of totally real fields Kⱼ over ℚ of degree 2^(j+1) and one root discriminant D for all natural j. It cites J. Martinet, *Tours de corps de classes et estimations de discriminants*, Invent. Math. 44 (1978), 65–73. The concrete D=4√70035 comes from a totally real unramified 2-tower over the degree-eight field ℚ(√2,√29,√(3·5·7·23·29)). Refining finite 2-extensions into quadratic steps gives all dyadic degrees starting at eight, keeping the root discriminant fixed. The concrete D cannot be the root discriminant at degree two since 16·70035 is not a quadratic fundamental discriminant. Cofinal dyadic degrees suffice for the final theorem. Alternatively the elementary small-degree argument at the end of this ledger treats the missing indices in Martinet’s example. The formalization uses a uniform upper bound on the root discriminant. Martinet’s explicit equal-discriminant tower is a distinct mathematical statement. MT.Tower.dyadic_totally_real_fields proves a uniform upper bound at every exact dyadic degree using Sawin’s formalized tower, which suffices for the argument. See [the checked field supply](tower.md). [Martinet](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01389902) · [Maire, §3.1](https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/~rehmann/ECM/cdrom/3ecm/pdfs/pant3/maire.pdf)
* **Integer lattice and norm (Appendix A, lemma:norminequalities).** Let σ=(σ₁,…,σₖ) list all k=[K:ℚ] real embeddings. The full rank lattice Λ=σ(𝒪_K) has basis matrix Mᵢⱼ=σᵢ(ωⱼ) for an integral basis. The norm and trace are the product and sum of embeddings, rational on K and integral on 𝒪_K. A nonzero algebraic integer has absolute norm ≥1. If the other conjugates have absolute value ≤T with T>0, then |σᵢ(x)|≥1/T^(k−1). For T≥1 this implies the paper's 1/Tᵏ. The two Lean lemmas are MT.Arithmetic.one_le_abs_rational_norm and MT.Arithmetic.conjugate_gap. A real infinite place evaluates the absolute value of its embedding.
* **Discriminant and dual.** MT.TracePairing.inner_embedding identifies the Euclidean inner product of two Minkowski embeddings with the rational field trace of their product. It uses the trace sum over embeddings, grouping the real places exactly once. The integral trace Gram matrix G=MᵀM has det G=disc K=(det M)²>0 independent of the integral basis; D=|disc K|^(1/k). Thus covol Λ=D^(k/2). Its Euclidean dual consists of ξ with integer scalar product against Λ. It equals M⁻ᵀℤᵏ, has covolume D^(−k/2), and lies in Mℚᵏ=σ(K) since M⁻ᵀ=MG⁻¹. For nonzero ξ∈Λᵛ, coordinatewise multiplication by ξ sends Λ into Λᵛ: test against a lattice point and use closure of the ring of integers under multiplication. Covolume comparison gives ∏|ξᵢ|≥D⁻ᵏ. No coordinate vanishes by injectivity of each embedding. MT.Arithmetic.dual_product_gap proves the product estimate for field elements with integral trace pairing against every algebraic integer. Its proof uses the principal fractional ideal times the different, whose integral ideal norm is positive. MT.Arithmetic.minkowski_dual_coordinate_gap applies the estimate to every nonzero vector of the geometric Euclidean dual via the trace-dual integral basis. MT.Arithmetic.minkowski_covolume identifies the volume factor.
* **Rectangle intersection (lemma:structureintersection).** For every a∈ℝᵏ and box Q=a+∏[−Mᵢ,Mᵢ], where every Mᵢ≥C₀D for a sufficiently large C₀(D), the ratio |Λ∩Q|/(vol(Q)/D^(k/2)) lies between b(D)⁻ᵏ and b(D)ᵏ. Integer norm implies unit separation in sup norm, so disjoint half-open unit cubes give the upper bound. For the lower bound choose ψ on [−1,1], between 0 and 1, integral 1, with |ψ̂(y)|≤c min(1,|y|⁻²); a triangular bump suffices. Poisson summation for the translated and dilated product has zero-frequency term ∏Mᵢ=2⁻ᵏvol(Q) before division by covolume. In each dyadic box 2^jᵢ<|Mᵢξᵢ|≤2^(jᵢ+1), sort first coordinates of N≥2 points to find a difference O(2^j₁/(NM₁)). Bounding other coordinate differences and applying the nonzero dual-product bound gives N≤b(D)ᵏ∏(2^jᵢ/Mᵢ). For a singleton apply the bound to the point itself. Empty boxes contribute zero. Summing uses ∑_{j∈ℤ}2^(j−2 max(j,0))=3. The error O_D(1)ᵏ is dominated by the zero-frequency term for C₀ large. The subsequent remark gives sharper 2^(−k−1), 2^(k+1) factors with an absolute C₀. MT.BoxCutoff.lattice_box_lower proves the explicit lower bound ∏Mᵢ/(2 covol Λ), with each Mᵢ≥48·MT.BoxCutoff.constant·D. MT.BoxCutoff.lattice_box_upper proves 1+2ᵏ∏Mᵢ by packing. MT.Arithmetic.fieldRect_lower and fieldRect_upper transport these estimates to the actual translated field-coordinate boxes. The compact orbit observation is unused.

## Seed and high-dimensional intersections

1. **Generic integer seed (lemma:seednoncollinearity).** For every integer m≥2 choose distinct integers 1≤aⱼ≤2^(3m). The subset sums ζ_J=(b_J,τ_J)=∑_{j∈J}P(aⱼ), indexed by subsets J⊂[m], must be pairwise distinct with no collinear triple. For three distinct subsets expand the determinant as ∑_{i≠j} cᵢⱼ aᵢ²aⱼ. The integer coefficient is the determinant with rows of j- and i-membership bits across the three subsets, and a row of ones. A nonconstant membership row has two equal entries. If all coefficients vanished these two columns would agree in every row, contradicting distinctness. The monomials are distinct. The product over unordered triples is a nonzero polynomial of total degree 3 binom(2ᵐ,3)<2^(3m). It cannot vanish on the entire Cartesian grid whose side cardinality is 2^(3m). A nonzero evaluation rules out coincident subset sums by using a third subset, and repeated aⱼ by using singletons. Coordinates are of size exp(O(m)); absolute nonzero integer determinants are ≥1. The optional lacunary-seed remark is unused.
2. **Seed energy.** A₀={aⱼ}, B₀={ζ_J}, |A₀|=m, |B₀|=2ᵐ. The maximum occupancy L₀ of nonvertical lines is ≤2. The m2^(m−1) labelled pairs (j,J) with j∉J satisfy P(aⱼ)+ζ_J=ζ_(J∪{j}). Write r(z) for the count of representations P(a)+ζ=z. Then ∑_{B₀}r≥m2^(m−1) and (∑_{B₀}r)²≤|B₀|E by Cauchy–Schwarz. Thus E≥(m²/4)|B₀| and E/(L₀|A₀||B₀|)≥m/8. To handle dyadic k<8 above, take m/8≥16, retain this finite seed for each such k and normalize by parabolic dilation. Positive finite minimum horizontal, vertical and determinant gaps permit one C large enough for all these k. By the strip estimate below, any such strip then contains at most two points. Use Lₖ=2.
3. **Product and thickening (lemma:seedrectangles).** Use (A₀ᵏ,B₀ᵏ), identifying (ℝ²)ᵏ with horizontal and vertical ℝᵏ factors. Choose ρ large in terms of D,m, at least max(2^(3m),2C₀D,2). Write H₀=C₀D for the source horizontal half-width, and take vertical half-width ρ^(3/2). Define Iⱼ=ρaⱼ+[−H₀,H₀], R_J=(ρb_J+[−H₀,H₀])×(ρ²τ_J+[−ρ^(3/2),ρ^(3/2)]), with respective unions U,V. Intervals are disjoint, rectangles are disjoint, every straight line meets at most two rectangles. After inverse parabolic dilation, errors are bounded by H₀/ρ,ρ^(−1/2). Finite noncollinear triples remain noncollinear under sufficiently small perturbations. The coordinate bounds allow ρ=exp(O_D(m)); the final theorem requires only finite dependence on D,m. Put 𝒜=Λ∩Uᵏ, ℬ=(Λ×Λ)∩Vᵏ.
4. **Sizes (claim1-04112026).** There are mᵏ disjoint boxes for 𝒜, each with all sides 2H₀. Lattice lower counts give |𝒜|≳≈mᵏ. There are 2^(mk) product boxes for ℬ, split into horizontal and vertical lattice counts. Lattice upper counts give |ℬ|≲≈(2ᵐρ^(3/2))ᵏ. The lower counts also ensure nonemptiness.
5. **Overlap (claim2-04112026, the live version after the comment).** For fixed a∈𝒪_K with σ(a)∈𝒜 write xᵢ=σᵢ(a)=ρa_(jᵢ)+uᵢ uniquely, |uᵢ|≤H₀. For Jᵢ⊂[m] excluding jᵢ, intersect R_(Jᵢ) with R_(Jᵢ∪{jᵢ})−P(xᵢ). Their centres differ by (−uᵢ,−2ρa_(jᵢ)uᵢ−uᵢ²), the vertical displacement bounded by ρ^(3/2) for ρ large. The overlap has **full** width ≥H₀ and full height ≥ρ^(3/2). To apply the box lemma with its **half**-side threshold, choose H₀=2C₀D initially and ρ^(3/2)≥2C₀D. This supplies the factor of two missing when the same C₀ is read literally throughout the manuscript. Each of the 2^((m−1)k) disjoint product intersections contains ≳≈ρ^(3k/2) product lattice points; adding P(σ(a)) stays in the lattice by ring closure. Thus |{ζ∈ℬ:P(σ(a))+ζ∈ℬ}|≳≈|ℬ|.
6. **Field energy (claim3-04112026).** Sum the preceding bound over 𝒜. The sum equals ∑_{z∈ℬ}r(z), while ∑_z r(z)²=E_K with finite support. Cauchy–Schwarz yields E_K≳≈|𝒜|²|ℬ|.
7. **Field lines (claim4-04112026 and lemma:energylowerbound).** For u,v∈K, the line τ=ub+v maps in the i-th embedding to the real line with slope σᵢ(u) and intercept σᵢ(v). It meets at most 2ᵏ product boxes. In each one the first coordinate determines the second over K, and the horizontal lattice box of sides 2H₀ contains ≲≈1 points. Hence maximum line occupancy L_K≤b(D)ᵏ. Combining the size, energy and line estimates gives E_K/(L_K|𝒜||ℬ|)≳≈mᵏ. Choose m=m(D) sufficiently large to make this ≥2ᵏ.

## Projection and small strips

8. Choose one real embedding σ₁ and S≥1 sufficiently large in terms of ρ. Send a to σ₁(a)/S and (b,τ) to (σ₁(b)/S,σ₁(τ)/S²), obtaining A,B in the prescribed unit boxes. Injectivity, additivity and multiplicativity induce bijections of sets and of ordered collision quadruples.
9. **Gaps (lemma:gaps).** Differences of coordinates and triple determinants before projection belong to 𝒪_K. Every conjugate has magnitude ≤T=ρ¹⁰⁰ after increasing ρ in terms of D,m; prior to dilation the horizontal subset sums are ≤m2^(3m) and vertical subset sums ≤m2^(6m). For each nonzero algebraic expression, the first absolute value is ≥ρ^(−100(k−1)). Scaling loses S⁻¹ for horizontal A differences, S⁻² for B vertical differences, and S⁻³ for doubled areas. Choose C=100S³ρ¹⁰⁰. Each nonzero expression after projection is ≥100C⁻ᵏ for k≥1. MT.Arithmetic.normalized_integral_gap checks this constant uniformly for scaling powers d≤3 from a bound T for the other conjugates. MT.Arithmetic.configuration_of_bounded_field_sets uses the bound 8M² for the determinants when all initial coordinates have every absolute conjugate at most M, with parabolic scaling S=M.
10. **Strips (lemma:stripoccupancy).** Put δ=C⁻ᵏ. For three points (xᵢ,yᵢ) with |xᵢ|≤1 and eᵢ=yᵢ−2sxᵢ in one closed interval of length δ, their doubled area is (x₂−x₁)(e₃−e₁)−(x₃−x₁)(e₂−e₁), of magnitude ≤4δ. The 100δ gap forces zero. Equal abscissas imply that the ordinates differ by at most δ and thus coincide. Two distinct preimages determine u=(τ₂−τ₁)/(b₂−b₁), v=τ₁−ub₁ in K. Every preimage lies on this field line because the projected doubled area equals σ₁(det_K)/S³. The empty and singleton cases admit horizontal lines. Thus occupancy is ≤L_K for every strip. The Lean declarations det_le_of_inStrip, vertical_eq_of_inStrip and strip_lifts_to_field_line in MT.Arithmetic cover this geometry. Its finite-cardinality conclusion, projected_strip_card_le, uses the same projectedPoint and strip filter as the configuration in the transfer proposition.
11. Set Lₖ=L_K. The three requirements of the amplification proposition follow. The explicit parameter choices and finite-set bounds appear below.

## Explicit losses in the formalized intersection argument

The code uses a bounding constant D≥1, so fields in different degrees need only satisfy |disc K|≤Dᵏ. Set V=ρ^(3/2) and s=√|disc K|. Choose H≥D with H/2≥48·MT.BoxCutoff.constant·D, followed by V≥H. Fix finite A and B as the unions over the actual field boxes. MT.Intersections.ATotal uses all mᵏ generator label tuples. MT.Intersections.BTotal uses the independent horizontal and vertical integral coordinates in each tuple of rectangles, with at most (2ᵐ)ᵏ tuples.

* **Generator count.** MT.Intersections.card_A_lower adds the sizes of the pairwise disjoint boxes. The raw estimate is |A|≥mᵏHᵏ/(2s). Since s²≤Dᵏ, D≤H and k≥1 this implies |A|≥(m/2)ᵏ.
* **Target count.** MT.Intersections.card_B_upper uses the union bound and the exact cardinality of a Cartesian product. Packing gives at most 1+(2r)ᵏ≤(4r)ᵏ points in a box of radius r≥1. Consequently |B|≤(2ᵐ)ᵏ(4H)ᵏ(4V)ᵏ.
* **Returning count.** MT.Intersections.card_return_lower selects the (2^(m−1))ᵏ tuples of subsets missing the return direction in each embedding. Each tuple supplies a translated product of boxes with radii H/2 and V/2 via MT.Seed.returning_rectangle. The labels distinguish points of different boxes. Translation in the ring of integers puts each counted point into the successor box. The number T of returning points for each a∈A satisfies T≥(2^(m−1))ᵏ(H/2)ᵏ(V/2)ᵏ/(4s²). MT.Intersections.return_base combines the two bounds and s²≤Dᵏ to give |B|≤(512D)ᵏT.
* **Line count.** Each real embedding permits at most two labels on a field line, giving at most 2ᵏ tuples. Its first coordinate determines each point on that field line, and the horizontal box contains at most (4H)ᵏ choices. Hence L≤(8H)ᵏ. A natural-number H permits the integer choice L=(8H)ᵏ directly.

MT.arithmeticAmplification_of_dyadic_fields checks the full deduction of Proposition 4.1 with a field of every required dyadic degree satisfying one fixed root-discriminant upper bound. MT.Tower.dyadic_totally_real_fields provides that premise at D=255255. The normalized losses cA=2, cR=512D, cL=8H are independent of the seed size and the dilation. MT.choose_seed_size chooses m≥2 such that 2cA cR² cL≤m. After Cauchy–Schwarz and the field-to-plane embedding, this inequality gives the required gain 2ᵏ.
